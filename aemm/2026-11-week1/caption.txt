Every year, hundreds of thousands of Nepalis leave for work in Malaysia and the Gulf. Before they go, they weigh two big things: how much they will earn, and how safe they will be.

Economist Maheshwor Shrestha interviewed 3,319 men applying for passports in Kathmandu in 2015. Some were shown real numbers on wages abroad, some were shown real numbers on worker deaths, and the researchers checked three months later who had actually gone.

The finding? We usually assume that people leaving for work abroad think only about the money and ignore the dangers. This study found that first-time migrants fear the dangers far too much. On average they guessed about 28 deaths per 1,000 workers over two years, when the real figure was about 1.3.

But they also expect far too much money. When they heard the true death numbers, migration among first-timers rose by about 7 percentage points. When they heard the true wages, it fell by about 6.

Why this matters: A decision this big rests on guesses, and the guesses can be wrong in both directions. Honest, simple facts on pay and safety, given before a worker signs a contract, help families decide better. This raises a bigger question about who should give migrants this information, and when.
