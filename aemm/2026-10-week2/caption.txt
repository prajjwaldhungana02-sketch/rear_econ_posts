On 26 August, the Bhotekoshi flood swept through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading, taking more than 1,300 lives. Seven weeks later, Rs 45,000 in cash support had reached about 4,100 families, while about 1,400 more were still waiting.

Does that delay matter? Researchers worked with the World Food Programme in 138 villages along the Karnali River and in Bangladesh. Some families got cash within days of a flood. Others got the same amount about six weeks later.

The finding? We usually assume that as long as flood victims get help, timing does not matter much. This study found it does. Families who got cash early ate better and felt less stressed than those who waited.

In Nepal, each family got Rs 15,000. Early cash raised their food score by 6% and meat eating by about 12%. In Bangladesh, early families saved 37% more and borrowed 17% less. A few months later both groups were about equal, so early help cost nothing later on.

Why this matters: Bhotekoshi families needed help in the first days, not the second month. Keeping cash and family lists ready before the monsoon costs no more than paying late, and it reaches families before they skip meals, sell animals or take costly loans.

💡 What do you think? After Bhotekoshi, should flood cash be kept ready so it reaches families within days, not weeks?
